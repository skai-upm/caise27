The application must be structured into at least the presentation, business, and persistence layers, with additional subpackages where necessary. Objective
Introduce invoice generation for users and groups by formally applying the Builder pattern.
Description
The application must generate invoices on demand from the financial information available in the system.
The following commands will be added:
INVOICE CREATE;USER;<correo>
INVOICE CREATE;GROUP;<identificadorGrupo>
An invoice must be built progressively using Builder, including only sections applicable to the recipient type and available information.
A user invoice may include individual tickets purchased directly for that user and personal passes associated with their tickets. Individual tickets belonging to a GROUPTICKET must not be counted again as individual purchases, although personal passes attached to them may appear.
A group invoice will include its GROUPTICKET purchases and their total amount, without adding the individual tickets composing them or the members' personal passes again.
Invoices are generated on demand and need not be persisted.
Acceptance criteria
• 
A Builder abstraction exists for constructing invoices.
• 
An invoice can be generated for an existing user.
• 
An invoice can be generated for an existing group.
• 
The invoice is assembled in distinct steps.
• 
Only sections applicable to the recipient and available data are included.
• 
Constructors with numerous optional parameters are not used to handle invoice variants.
• 
Internal tickets of a GROUPTICKET are not counted twice.
• 
Personal passes associated with internal tickets may appear on the user's invoice.
• 
Invoices display the prices of included items and a total.
• 
Invoices do not need to be persisted.

Objective
Formalize the uniqueness of controller classes and configure collaboration among components through dependency injection.
Description
Controller classes representing a single management responsibility within the application must guarantee a single instance through the Singleton pattern.
Persistence repositories must not be considered single instances. The design must assume that an application may use different repository implementations or configurations. Necessary instances must be created/configured at startup and injected into the components that use them.
When a controller needs to collaborate with another controller or service, the dependency must be configured during application initialization rather than constructed locally inside the operation using it.
Acceptance criteria
• 
Controllers that are conceptually unique apply Singleton.
• 
Repositories are not required to be implemented as Singletons.
• 
Repositories are created/configured at startup and injected.
• 
Normal and --fly modes can use repositories with different paths without changing controller logic.
• 
Required dependencies among controllers are established during initialization.

Objective
Add personal passes associated with individual tickets, including prices, persistence, consumption, and refunds.
Description
An individual ticket may have multiple SpeedPasses and at most one Special Pass throughout its lifetime.
SpeedPasses may be purchased individually or in packs of ten. Each SpeedPass is an independent object with an identifier, price, and consumption status. Its price is 20% of the price stored in its associated individual ticket.
The Special Pass costs the same as the price stored in its associated individual ticket. Only one may be purchased for a given individual ticket, even if it has subsequently been used.
Consumed passes remain in the system with their corresponding status. Unused passes may be refunded when the date of the associated ticket permits a refund under E2's time-related rules.
Acceptance criteria
• 
SPEEDPASS CREATE accepts quantities of 1 and 10.
• 
A purchase of ten generates ten independent SpeedPasses.
• 
Each SpeedPass stores its own price and consumption status.
• 
SPECIALPASS CREATE permits at most one Special Pass per individual ticket.
• 
A SpeedPass costs 20% of the associated ticket price.
• 
The Special Pass price equals the associated ticket price.
• 
Purchases display the amount charged.
• 
SPEEDPASS DELETE and SPECIALPASS DELETE allow unused passes to be refunded when permitted.
• 
Consumed passes are not removed upon use.

Objective
Extend ATTRACTION ENTER to use SpeedPasses and Special Passes while respecting atomicity, attraction type, and consumption rules.
Description
At GENERAL and INDIVIDUAL attractions, an individual ticket may enter normally or request use of a SpeedPass with the SPEED option.
GENERAL and GROUP attractions also permit group ticket entry with SPEED. In that case, every individual ticket in the composition must have at least one unused SpeedPass. If any member lacks one or any other access condition fails, no user enters and no SpeedPass is consumed.
SPECIAL attractions permit individual entries only. The system automatically locates the Special Pass associated with the ticket, checks that it has not been consumed, and marks it as consumed only after successful entry. The SPEED option is not accepted at these attractions.
Acceptance criteria
• 
ATTRACTION ENTER;<atraccion>;<entrada> preserves normal entry.
• 
ATTRACTION ENTER;<atraccion>;<entrada>;SPEED requests SpeedPass use.
• 
Individual entry with SPEED consumes exactly one unused SpeedPass.
• 
Group entry with SPEED consumes one for each member.
• 
Group entry with SPEED is atomic.
• 
A failed entry does not consume passes.
• 
A SPECIAL attraction automatically consumes its Special Pass.
• 
The output reports when a Special Pass or SpeedPass is consumed.
• 
SPEED is rejected at SPECIAL attractions.

Objective
Persist the new E3 state and update the PlantUML model.
Description
Persistence must reconstruct passes, their prices, their associated individual tickets, and consumed status. It must also retain the type of each attraction.
When a future individual ticket is validly deleted, all associated SpeedPasses and its Special Pass must be cascade-deleted. When a future group ticket is validly deleted, passes associated with each individual ticket in its composition must be deleted first, then those individual tickets, and finally the group ticket.
The uml/model.puml file must be updated to represent the new classes, interfaces, generics, associations, and relevant relationships.
Acceptance criteria

• 
The consumed status is preserved.
• 
Each pass's price is preserved.
• 
The attraction type is preserved.
• 
TICKET DELETE cascade-deletes associated passes.
• 
GROUPTICKET DELETE consistently removes passes, constituent tickets, and the group ticket.
• 
A failed deletion leaves no partial deletions.
• 
--fly also applies to the new data.
• 
uml/model.puml reflects the actual E3 design.
